How to prepare for your test

Correct preparation gives accurate results. Follow your doctor's instructions first — this guide covers the general rules used by most laboratories.

{{icon:clipboard}}Before any test

  • Bring your prescription, a photo ID and previous reports.
  • Wear a loose sleeve that rolls up easily.
  • Drink normal amounts of water unless told otherwise — it makes blood collection easier.
  • Tell us if you are pregnant, on blood thinners, diabetic, or have fainted during a blood test before.
  • Avoid heavy exercise and alcohol for 24 hours before blood tests.

{{icon:clock}}Fasting tests

No food or drinks except plain water for the time below. No tea, coffee, milk, juice, chewing gum or smoking.

  • Fasting blood sugar (FBS): at least 8 hours (usually 8–12).
  • Lipid profile: 10–12 hours only if your doctor asks; many routine checks can be done without fasting.
  • Fasting insulin, C-peptide, homocysteine: usually 8–12 hours.
  • Post-prandial sugar (PP): eat your normal meal and give the sample 2 hours after you start eating.
DoBook an early-morning slot. Diabetics: ask your doctor how to adjust insulin or tablets while fasting.
Don'tFast for more than 14 hours — it can affect results and make you feel unwell.

{{icon:flask}}Glucose tolerance test (GTT / OGTT)

  • Eat normal meals with carbohydrates (rice, chapati) for 3 days before the test.
  • Fast for at least 8 hours overnight; water is allowed.
  • A fasting sample is taken, then you drink 75 g glucose in water within 5 minutes.
  • Further samples are taken at the times your doctor ordered (commonly 1 and 2 hours).
  • Stay seated at the lab. Do not eat, smoke or walk around during the test. Allow 2–3 hours.

If you vomit after the glucose drink, tell the staff — the test may need to be rescheduled.

{{icon:drop}}Urine sample

  • Use only the sterile container from the lab. Don't touch the inside of the cap or cup.
  • First morning urine is preferred for routine, albumin and pregnancy tests.
  • Wash hands and clean the genital area with water.
  • Pass the first part into the toilet, collect the middle part, finish in the toilet.
  • Close the lid tightly and bring it within 1 hour.
  • For culture, give the sample before starting antibiotics unless your doctor says otherwise.
  • Women: tell us if you are menstruating; it can affect the result.

{{icon:germ}}Stool sample

  • Pass stool onto a clean, dry surface (such as clean paper or a container), not directly into the toilet water.
  • Do not let urine or water mix with the sample.
  • Use the spoon in the lab container to take a small amount — about the size of a pea or a teaspoon if liquid. Pick areas with blood or mucus if present.
  • Bring it to the lab within 1 hour.

{{icon:micro}}Semen analysis

  • Avoid ejaculation for 2 to 7 days before the test.
  • Collect the complete sample by masturbation into the sterile lab container.
  • Do not use a condom, lubricant, saliva or soap.
  • Collecting at the lab is best. If collected at home, bring it within 1 hour, kept close to body temperature (for example in an inner pocket).
  • Tell us if any part of the sample was spilled.

{{icon:calendar}}Hormones and timed tests

  • FSH, LH (fertility): usually day 2–5 of the menstrual cycle (day 1 = first day of bleeding), or as your doctor advises.
  • AMH: any day of the cycle.
  • Prolactin: morning, a few hours after waking, after resting quietly.
  • Cortisol: morning sample around 8–9 AM, or the time on your prescription.
  • Testosterone (men): morning, ideally before 10 AM.
  • Dual marker: 11 to 13 weeks 6 days of pregnancy. Triple / Quad marker: usually 15–20 weeks. Bring your latest scan report.
  • Mantoux: return for the reading 48–72 hours after the injection.
  • PSA: avoid ejaculation and vigorous cycling for 48 hours before.

{{icon:alert}}Medicines and supplements

Do not stop any prescribed medicine on your own. Ask your doctor, and tell the lab what you take — especially:

  • Blood thinners (warfarin, acenocoumarol, heparin) — for PT-INR and APTT, note the time of your last dose.
  • Thyroxine — ask your doctor whether to take it before or after the thyroid test.
  • Biotin / hair-skin-nail supplements — high doses can falsely change thyroid and some hormone results.
  • Iron tablets — may need to be paused for 24 hours before iron studies, if your doctor agrees.
  • Steroids, hormonal pills and B12 injections — tell the lab.

{{icon:user}}Children and elderly patients

  • Children should not fast unless the doctor specifically asks. Bring a snack for after the sample.
  • A parent or guardian must accompany patients under 18.
  • Elderly or diabetic patients on a fasting test should bring something to eat right after the sample.
  • Tell us in advance about mobility needs.

Emergency warning signs: chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, confusion, heavy bleeding, or high fever with bleeding spots need immediate care. Go to the nearest hospital or call 108 — do not wait for a laboratory test.
